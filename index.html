    Rule changes the agent made, always inside the owner's limits

      What the test group finds

      Paying even a small amount may make guests more likely to show up (the commitment effect). The agent can't control this; it measures it with the 15% test group and changes its own action if the evidence is clear. Illustrative scenarios:
      Headline results above assume no commitment effect, so they're conservative.

      Simulation assumptions

      • About 31% of bookings are direct and not prepaid (hotel operator interview).
      • Base no-show risk: Trusted 2%, Standard 9%, Watch 24%, adjusted by channel, lead time, night demand, group size and long stays. Calibrated so today's process gives about 5.3%. Benchmarks: pay-at-hotel 5–10% vs prepaid 1–2%.
      • When asked for an advance, of the guests who weren't going to come: 35% cancel, 12% pay but still don't come, and 53% go silent (and can be released on busy or medium nights).
      • When sent a reconfirmation, 50% of those guests tap Cancel.
      • Walk-away when asked: token 3%, first night 6%. ×0.5 on busy nights, ×1.4 on quiet nights, ×1.5 if booked ≤2 days ahead.
      • Resale of a room freed early: busy 75%, medium 45%, quiet 10%. Freed at 6 PM on arrival day: 20% / 8% / 2%.
      • Failed payments: 9% of attempts. The agent's fresh link recovers 75%; staff today recover 35%.
      • Treebo case (Razorpay): a 20% advance via payment links cut no-shows by 30%. Indians book hotels about 5 days ahead on average.
      • Guest tiers use only this hotel's own history. In production, base no-show rates could be calibrated from anonymised, aggregate outcomes across hotels; no guest identities are shared.
